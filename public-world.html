How Aldernia knows

Confidence is not a source.

Real-world claims should be traceable to competent authority and evidence. Fiction should admit that it is fiction. Interpretation should not dress itself up as fact.

  1. Source factWhat a competent source actually establishes.
  2. Attributed claimWhat a named person or institution says.
  3. InferenceWhat reasonably follows, with uncertainty visible.
  4. Editorial judgementAnalysis or opinion that is clearly presented as such.
  5. FictionIn-world Aldernian material, never disguised as real evidence.
  6. UnknownWhen the evidence does not settle the question.

Currentness

Old information should not wear today's clothes.

“Today” must be earned, not achieved by changing a date.

EditorialPublished material with a clear date and material updates where needed.
CurrentOperational information with an explicit “as at” state and responsible source.
ScheduledA future event or change with a real date, time and status.
ReferenceDurable material reviewed periodically rather than pretending to be live.
LiveReserved for information genuinely connected to a verified current source.

Values in public

Convictions may speak clearly. They may not hide.

Catholic contribution

Human dignity, the common good, subsidiarity, solidarity, conscience, religious freedom, peace and reconciliation may shape explicit moral reflection. Catholic teaching is identified as Catholic where that provenance matters; belief remains voluntary.

British civic inheritance

Scrutiny, independent institutions, parliamentary habit, regional pluralism, public service, archives, voluntary associations, understatement and dry humour influence how Aldernia argues and governs — not what a visitor is secretly required to believe.

Peace

Aldernia should help people understand conflict earlier, recognise dehumanisation and propaganda, learn about diplomacy, humanitarian law, reconciliation and reconstruction, and gain more tools for peaceful resolution.

Humans and AI

The same public truth.

If a fact matters to an AI answering from Aldernia, it should also be findable and interpretable by a human.

Future AI may help retrieve, explain, quiz, compare or summarise approved public material. It does not become the source of a fact because it sounds confident.

AI may assist.

It may not invent customers, witnesses, evidence, official authority, public sentiment, votes, expert consensus or real-world relationships.
