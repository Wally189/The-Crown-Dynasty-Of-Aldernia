The founding settlement

Power should have an address—and a limit.

Aldernia is a fictional parliamentary Crown state with a written Constitution, a 120-seat Assembly, independent courts and watchdogs, a professional Civil Service and seven elected regions.

Fictional constitutional order

The people are not scenery.

The public civic electorate is designed to ratify the Constitution and later elect the Assembly. It is not open yet.

The Crown

Continuity, not day-to-day government

King Alan is the in-world head of state; Queen Barbara is Constitutional Steward. The Crown appoints the Prime Minister who wins Assembly confidence and normally assents to properly enacted law.

The Assembly

120 seats · one chamber

It makes laws, approves tax and spending, scrutinises Government, receives petitions and can remove a Government through the formal confidence process.

Government

Ten accountability homes

Political ministers choose lawful priorities. A permanent impartial Civil Service carries evidence, implementation, records and protected professional challenge.

Government formation

No palace mathematics.

  1. General election

    The outgoing Government enters caretaker status.

  2. Assembly meets

    A candidate for Prime Minister must win a recorded confidence vote.

  3. 61 votes

    A majority of the whole 120-seat Assembly establishes confidence.

  4. Crown appointment

    The Crown appoints the person who has demonstrated confidence; it does not substitute a preferred candidate.

If formal confidence is later lost, the Assembly has 14 days to support an alternative Prime Minister before an election follows.

Hierarchy

What outranks what?

Constitution → Acts → authorised regulations → regional/local bylaws → administrative decisions → guidance.

Guidance is not law. Policy is not law. Crown preference is not law. A newspaper editorial is definitely not law, however cross it sounds.

Local democracy

Not everything belongs in St Aurelia.

Seven elected regional councils handle strategic transport, regional planning, skills, development and cross-council infrastructure where law assigns it. Cities, towns and districts handle local services. Optional community councils can manage genuinely local matters.

The rule is subsidiarity: put a decision at the lowest level that can competently carry it without undermining rights, national standards or cross-boundary systems.
