Experiment Journal

What actually happened?

The Experiment explains what I am doing.

The Hypothesis explains what I think might happen.

This is where I record what actually does.

The Experiment Journal is the running story of Aldernia as an experiment: what I tried, what changed, what surprised me, what failed, what worked and what I learned from it.

It is deliberately more human than a formal research record.

Experiments happen through false starts, sudden ideas, irritating defects, small discoveries and occasions when something that looked extremely clever on Tuesday appears completely absurd by Friday.

Those things belong here too.

Evidence before mythology

Aldernia is capable of generating an extraordinary amount of apparent activity.

That creates a danger.

A fictional country can make almost anything look as though something important has happened.

So the Journal distinguishes between what happened inside the experiment and what has actually been demonstrated beyond it.

Something working inside Aldernia is evidence about Aldernia.

A simulation succeeding is evidence about that simulation.

Neither automatically proves that something works for a real customer, learner, organisation or member of the public.

Where something crosses that boundary, I will say so.

Where it has not, I will say that too.

The awkward bits belong here.

An experiment in which everything works would be extremely suspicious.

So this Journal will include things I got wrong.

Ideas which became unnecessarily complicated.

Rules which solved one problem and created another.

Times when AI confidently invented something it should not have.

Times when I became the bottleneck.

Times when the machinery became bureaucracy.

Times when the simpler answer won.

And occasions when something genuinely unexpected worked remarkably well.

Those may be the most useful entries.

Sometimes the conclusion will simply be:

I still don't know.

That is a perfectly respectable result.

Aldernia will change.

Its institutions will develop.

Some things will disappear.

Rules will be revised.

Ideas that currently seem fundamental may eventually look rather quaint.

The Journal does not describe a finished system.

It records a system learning what it is.

There will be no attempt to tidy the story afterwards and pretend the route was obvious.

It wasn't.

It still isn't.

That is why I am keeping the Journal.

20 September 2026 · Opening note

Will it?

I was tinkering with ChatGPT after a particularly annoying day at work. In my wisdom, I randomly asked it to help me build an AI venture business.

As I started building it, because of the way my mind works, I kept finding myself creating tools to solve different problems. At first, a lot of those tools were quite abstract, so I started cross-comparing them with real-world examples to see whether they actually made sense.

One thing led to another and, before I really knew what had happened, I had built an entire ecosystem around the business. A lot of it was based on what I already knew from real life — organisations, departments, governance, people doing different jobs, systems interacting with one another, and the various things that make institutions actually work.

The strange thing is that I still don't really know whether this ecosystem is something fanciful that exists mainly in my head, or whether it is genuinely working.

But it has achieved something.

I now have a functioning business website and a business plan. Things that began as ideas have turned into actual, tangible things. So whatever this ecosystem is, it clearly isn't doing nothing.

And that has made me want to document the journey.

Not because I know where it is going, but precisely because I don't. I want to see what happens if I keep developing it, testing it against reality and seeing where the whole thing leads.

Maybe I'm mad as a box of frogs.

But this is Aldernia.

The online country I imagined where this ecosystem can work at its best: supporting my business, helping ideas become real and, perhaps, helping to achieve some genuinely great and good things along the way.

There is really only one question.

Will it?
