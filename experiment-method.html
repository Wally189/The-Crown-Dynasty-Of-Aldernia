The Aldernian Experiment

The Experiment

The interesting question stopped being “can AI answer this?” and became “what happens when different kinds of AI-assisted work have institutions, memory, boundaries and somewhere to interact?”

Boxes and arrows only get you so far.

Once House of Carol began to resemble an organisation, another problem became impossible to ignore.

Businesses do not exist by themselves. Neither do people.

They sit inside an extraordinary web of institutions which we mostly take for granted because, when they work properly, they are almost invisible: banks, schools, universities, government, hospitals, courts, newspapers, churches, standards bodies and infrastructure.

House of Carol could not really be tested as though none of that existed.

So I wondered: what happens if I build some of the surrounding institutional world as well?

Not real institutions. Simulations and structures: places where different kinds of questions could live, with different responsibilities and limits.

Government questions would go somewhere concerned with government. Finance somewhere concerned with finance. Education somewhere concerned with learning and research. Media somewhere capable of challenging everybody else.

Crucially, they would not all be the same enormous all-purpose AI wearing different hats.

Then came the Dynasty.

Institutions appeared. Then relationships between institutions. Then questions about who could decide what, and what happened when they disagreed.

Which is roughly the point at which you rediscover several thousand years of political history in miniature.

If institutions have powers, somebody has to define them. If decisions are made, somebody has to record them. If governments exist, somebody ought to be able to challenge them.

And if everything ultimately belongs to one human being sitting in Bristol with a mobile phone, that fact probably ought to appear somewhere in the constitutional arrangements.

At some point I observed, jokingly, that given the growing collection of Houses and institutions, I appeared to be King.

Unfortunately the joke was structurally useful.

So it stayed. That is how a man trying to build an AI venture business accidentally acquired a Crown Dynasty.

Eventually I needed a world.

The institutions interacted. They shared rules. One could produce something another needed. One could challenge another. A decision in one place could have consequences somewhere else.

What I needed was not another organisational chart.

I needed a world.

And so Aldernia emerged.

Not as a claim to sovereignty, and certainly not because I had developed territorial ambitions towards South Gloucestershire.

Aldernia is a way of making the system comprehensible: a place where the different pieces can exist, interact and be tested.

The question

If you give artificial intelligence something resembling institutions, responsibilities, memory, boundaries, evidence and a world in which all of those things have to interact — does it become more useful?

I do not know the answer.

That is the experiment.

Some of it is deliberately fictional. Some of it touches the real world. House of Carol, for example, is a genuine business project. That does not prove Aldernia works. A website is not evidence of a functioning civilisation.

But things have crossed the boundary from conversation into reality, which makes the question worth continuing to test.
