The Aldernian Experiment

The Aldernian Experiment

An experiment begins with a question.

You have an idea about what might be true. You find a way to test it. You observe what actually happens, record the evidence, change something where necessary and try again.

Most importantly, you do not decide the answer beforehand.

Aldernia is my experiment.

It is not a laboratory experiment, and I am not pretending that it is. It is a practical, long-running experiment in building and observing a digital world in which artificial intelligence operates through different institutions, responsibilities, memories and boundaries.

I want to find out what happens.

Why am I doing it?

Several reasons.

To learn

Artificial intelligence is developing extraordinarily quickly.

I am much more interested in understanding it by building with it than by simply reading predictions about what it might eventually do.

Aldernia gives me somewhere to experiment, make mistakes, notice unexpected things and learn from what happens.

To share what I learn

Discussion about AI often rushes towards the extremes: it will solve everything, destroy everything, replace everybody or somehow become irrelevant by next Thursday.

Reality is likely to be more interesting.

What works? What fails? What becomes unexpectedly useful? What becomes ridiculous? Where does human judgement matter? What happens when different AI-assisted institutions disagree?

Those seem like questions worth exploring in public.

To support my business

House of Carol is a real business project.

Aldernia gives me a wider environment in which ideas, systems, research and ways of working can be developed and challenged.

But there is an important boundary.

A simulated institution can test an idea.

It cannot invent a customer.

Internal success is not market evidence. A convincing document is not revenue. If Aldernia helps House of Carol become a better real business, that will have to be demonstrated in reality.

To explore whether AI can be a force for good

Not because AI is automatically good.

It is not.

Technology inherits purposes, decisions, limits and mistakes from the people and institutions surrounding it.

One thing I want to explore is whether AI becomes more useful when people remain visibly responsible for what it is being asked to do: setting the purpose, defining boundaries, checking evidence, correcting mistakes and deciding when the machine should not decide at all.

AI does not make human responsibility disappear.

Aldernia is partly an attempt to discover what happens when you design around that fact.

And because it is fun

This may be the least complicated part of the methodology.

I enjoy building it.

I enjoy watching one apparently sensible idea cause three completely unexpected problems somewhere else.

And I enjoy the fact that trying to organise some AI-assisted work eventually required government, journalism, banking, education, constitutional law and, somewhat accidentally, a King.

Curiosity is a perfectly respectable reason to conduct an experiment.

How does the experiment work?

The method is deliberately simple.

Build

Create something with a defined purpose: an institution, responsibility, rule, relationship, public artefact or way of working.

Test

Give it something meaningful to do.

Use real questions and evidence where appropriate, and controlled simulations where reality is unnecessary or inappropriate.

Observe

See what actually happens.

Does the system remember? Does work reach the right place? Do different institutions really behave differently? Can one challenge another? Does a new rule solve a problem — or merely create more administration?

Record

Keep the evidence, including the awkward bits.

Successes matter.

So do failures, contradictions, duplicated work, invented certainty and occasions when the correct answer turns out to be “stop”.

If I record only the impressive bits, I am not conducting much of an experiment.

I am doing marketing.

Change

Where the evidence exposes a weakness, change something.

Preferably not everything at once.

Then test again.

Repeat

Build. Test. Observe. Record. Change. Repeat.

Aldernia is intended to develop through that cycle rather than towards a predetermined finished state.

I do not know exactly what it will become.

That uncertainty is not a defect in the experiment.

It is why the experiment exists.
