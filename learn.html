House of Tony · public learning

A country should keep learning after school.

Play at four. Algebra at fourteen. A new trade at forty. A museum on Saturday. A difficult question at eighty. Aldernia treats learning as part of ordinary life.

Aldernian fiction

The principle

Leave every stage with more knowledge, more agency, more capability and more routes open than when you entered.

Learning through life

One ecology. Many ways through it.

Aldernia does not divide childhood education, technical skill, universities, adult learning, libraries and public knowledge into separate planets.

Birth–5

Start well

Language, relationships, movement, imagination, early number and literacy through safe, play-rich early years.

5–11

Build foundations

Reading, writing and mathematics alongside science, culture, making, movement and curiosity.

11–16

Broaden and deepen

Strong subject knowledge without forcing teenagers to choose between being “academic” and being “practical”.

16–18

Choose a route

Sixth form, technical college, apprenticeship or supported work-with-learning — with independent advice and real alternatives.

18+

Keep routes open

University, advanced technical learning, work, modular study, professional learning and retraining across adult life.

Any age

Learn because you can

Libraries, museums, public lectures, sport, music, craft, local history and the occasional topic that has no business case at all.

Schools

Foundations without narrowing the world.

Reading, writing and mathematics matter enormously. They are foundations, not an excuse to demolish everything built above them.

Inclusive Learning Guarantee

Design the system to include people.

  • Early identification before difficulty becomes crisis.
  • Capable mainstream provision with accessible teaching and reasonable support.
  • Specialist provision where specialist support is genuinely needed.
  • Child and family voice with a named route to review and appeal.

Teachers

A profession, not a paperwork delivery system.

Preparation combines subject knowledge, pedagogy, supervised practice, safeguarding, assessment, inclusion and digital literacy. Career routes allow excellent teachers to progress without having to stop teaching.

New reporting requirements must justify the information they demand. Administrative burden is treated as a design problem, not a character-building exercise.

Routes at 16 and beyond

Parity of esteem does not require fake equivalence.

Academic and technical routes can be equally respected while proving different kinds of capability.

01

Academic

Subject depth, inquiry and progression into higher study or work.

02

Technical

Occupational standards, real equipment, applied knowledge and independently assessed competence.

03

Apprenticeship

Paid work plus structured learning, employer participation and independent assessment.

04

Supported transition

Learning, work preparation and support designed around capability rather than a label of failure.

Careers information should show entry requirements, content, costs or finance, workplace expectations, destinations and credible alternatives — independently of provider recruitment incentives.

Aldernian Learning Account

Finance should open routes, not quietly close them.

A fictional public entitlement supports eligible higher and advanced technical study across working life. Foundational adult literacy, numeracy and essential digital learning sit outside the finite entitlement.

Fictional policy, not financial advice: the current Stage 05 world model uses a planning maximum of AC 6,500 per full-time-equivalent year for eligible Level 4–6 provision. No real payment, loan or student-finance product is offered here.

Science & research

How Aldernia tries to know something.

The Aldernian Research & Discovery Council is a fictional arm’s-length public funder. Public money is accountable; individual grant choices are not a minister’s personal reading list.

  1. QuestionWhat are we trying to find out?
  2. MethodCould this approach actually answer it?
  3. Ethics & dataIs it lawful, proportionate and safe?
  4. ObserveCollect evidence without moving the goalposts.
  5. AnalyseReport uncertainty, not just significance.
  6. ChallengePeer review, replication and contrary evidence matter.
  7. PublishAs open as possible; as closed as necessary.
  8. CorrectA finding may survive, change or fail.
Science journalism rule: a university press release is a source, not proof. House of Josie remains free to ask what was measured, how, with what uncertainty, what conflicts existed and whether anybody has replicated it.

Libraries · museums · arts · sport

Human development includes beautiful, useful and gloriously non-economic things.

Libraries

Books, children’s reading, digital access, study space, local history, accessible formats, skills signposting, lectures, making and community memory.

Museums

Collections with provenance, conservation, public access, learning and honest treatment of contested history rather than a room full of confident labels.

Arts

Arm’s-length funding and regional access. Every child should meet real cultural practice: a performance, a museum or gallery, a maker, a writer, a library and local culture.

Sport

Movement for health, joy, skill, teamwork, belonging, fair play and community — not simply a national medal production line.

Public knowledge

Teach. Explain. Investigate. Do not merge the jobs.

Government may explain policy, funding, services and official guidance.

House of Tony may teach methods, courses and public knowledge.

Universities and ARDC may publish research and public engagement.

House of Josie remains editorially independent and may investigate school failure, research misconduct, university finance or bad claims.

Rule: public learning is not a permission slip for propaganda.

Common Knowledge

Start somewhere useful.

EvidenceHow to read a claim

Ask exactly what is being claimed, what evidence could change your mind, what the source can actually know, and how much uncertainty remains.

ScienceHow to read a study

Begin with the question, population, comparison, outcome and limitations. The headline comes later.

MoneyCompound interest, minus the mysticism

Growth on previous growth matters in both savings and debt. The arithmetic is simpler than the consequences.

CivicsWhy constitutions need procedures

“Be fair” is a principle. Who counts votes, resolves disputes and publishes the result is a procedure.

InformationWhy correction can increase trust

Reliable institutions are not those that never err. They make errors discoverable, correctable and hard to repeat.

WorkWhy spreadsheets become organisational problems

The spreadsheet is rarely the villain. Unclear ownership, duplicate truth and invisible handoffs usually deserve questioning first.
