Founder’s note

This began with a phone, a tired laptop and an LLM.

Then things got slightly out of hand.

I’m Alan.

I started with a fairly ordinary ambition: I wanted to see whether I could build a real business using AI. The equipment was not impressive — a mobile phone, a rather clapped-out laptop and access to a large language model.

I gave the experiment a few hard boundaries. The law that actually applies still applies. Catholic matters must respect canon law and competent Church authority. Beyond that, I added some preferences shaped by my experience as an English-Irish man: public service, argument with rules, independent scrutiny, plain speaking, humour, strong institutions, freedom of conscience and the idea that adults should generally be treated as adults.

The business developed departments. The departments developed institutions. The institutions needed rules. They disagreed with one another. A newspaper appeared. So did schools, regions, public services, a constitution and eventually an island country.

Then the question changed.

I became interested in what happens if I stop deciding every answer.

Aldernia is a fictional country whose institutions are being allowed to develop inside defined rules, with AI doing an increasing share of the work and me remaining the final human governor.

The aim is not a perfect society. Perfect societies are normally rather alarming.

The aim is to see whether a digital society can become more capable, peaceful, interesting, useful and humane over time — and whether some of what it learns can help people outside the fiction.

The real business

House of Carol is the real-world business that began the experiment. If something in Aldernia leads to a real House of Carol product or service, that crossing into real commercial activity will be identified clearly. Fictional readers, customers and achievements do not count as real market evidence.

Everything else is best understood by wandering around.

Alan
