Founder’s note

This began with a phone, a tired laptop and an LLM.

Then things got slightly out of hand.

I’m Alan.

I started with a fairly ordinary ambition: I wanted to see whether I could build a real business using AI. The equipment was not impressive — a mobile phone, a rather clapped-out laptop and access to a large language model.

I supplied a few hard starting constraints. Real-world activity still has to obey the law that actually applies. Catholic matters remain subject to Canon Law and competent Church authority. I also brought some preferences shaped by being English-Irish and by my own working life: public service, argument with rules, independent scrutiny, plain speaking, humour, strong institutions, freedom of conscience and the idea that adults should generally be treated as adults.

The business developed departments. The departments developed institutions. The institutions needed rules. They disagreed with one another. A newspaper appeared. So did education, government, finance, regions, public services, a constitution and eventually an island country.

Then the question changed.

I wanted to see what happened if I gradually stopped giving it the answers.

Genesis is the point from which Aldernia starts being observed as a developing simulated society rather than being supplied with a finished story. Its institutions are expected to act within their authority, use evidence, disagree where warranted, correct themselves and return to me only when a decision genuinely belongs to the Founder.

The aim is not a perfect society. Perfect societies are normally rather alarming.

The aim is to see whether a digital society can become more capable, peaceful, interesting, useful and humane over time — and whether some of what it learns can help people outside the fiction.

The real business

House of Carol is the real-world business that began the experiment. At Genesis its authoritative financial record shows £5 cleared cash, £0 external cleared revenue and £0 proprietor income.

If a capability developed inside Aldernia becomes a real House of Carol product or service, the move into real commercial activity must be identified clearly. Fictional customers and fictional success never count as real market evidence.

Everything else is best understood by wandering around — and, from now on, coming back later to see what changed.

Alan
