Aldernian character

Stone, sea, alder, paper, brass.

Aldernia is an old island country of limestone civic buildings, working harbours, gardens and forests, ferries and trams: ceremonial when ceremony matters, modern without making technology the national costume.

Its visual language is meant to feel inhabited: modern buildings beside older streets, public information beside weather, culture beside administration, and enough regional difference that the country never becomes one template with seven labels.

National character

Serve well. Speak plainly.

Aldernia aims to be civilised without becoming sterile; competent without becoming pompous; traditional enough to possess memory and modern enough to repair a bad system.

Its public culture values service, liberty of conscience, learning, neighbourliness, institutional independence, correction and the slightly radical expectation that power should explain itself.

Seven regions

One realm, several opinions about the weather.

Crown Capital Territory 1.28m

St Aurelia, the River Aure, Parliament, the Crown and the nation’s densest concentration of people who know a committee clerk.

Northmere 1.07m

Greyspine mountains, engineering, advanced manufacturing, technical education and the road to Mount Caerwyn.

Bracken Coast 0.80m

Western ports, fisheries, cliffs, storm weather and a literary culture that has had ample opportunity to observe rain.

Eastvale 0.96m

Farms, orchards, old market towns and fertile river settlements.

Southmarch 1.12m

Ports, shipyards, beaches, mixed industry and newer towns.

High Alder 0.85m

Forests, water, reservoirs, renewable energy and long-distance walking country.

St Brigid’s & Outer Isles 0.32m

Maritime communities, old religious foundations, ferries, birdlife and a measured suspicion of instructions issued in St Aurelia.
