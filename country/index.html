The country in one minute

Old places. Young constitution.

Aldernia’s modern state is young; its towns, monasteries, ports, guilds, universities and grudges are not.

The contemporary realm begins with the Aldernian Compact: a peaceful decision by the regions to create common democratic institutions while preserving strong local identities. The Crown supplies continuity and ceremony. Government governs. Parliament legislates and scrutinises. Independent institutions are supposed to make life awkward for everyone at exactly the moments when that is useful.

National character

Serve well. Speak plainly.

Aldernia aims to be civilised without becoming sterile; competent without becoming pompous; traditional enough to possess memory and modern enough to repair a bad system. Its public culture values service, liberty of conscience, learning, neighbourliness, institutional independence, correction and the slightly radical expectation that power should explain itself.

It is not a disguised copy of any real country. It borrows tested democratic mechanisms, but its habits, geography and arguments are its own.
