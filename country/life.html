Ordinary Aldernia

The country between official announcements.

Most of national life is not a constitutional crisis. It is breakfast, school runs, late trains, Sunday worship, shift changes, football, shopping, a ferry timetable and someone trying to remember whether the recycling goes out tonight.

Today in Aldernia
The living-country system uses real time as context, not as permission to manufacture events.

A normal week

Institutions are credible when ordinary life fits around them.

Education

Local schools, technical colleges, universities and adult learning sit across the regions. Northmere leans technical; St Brigid’s has an old university-college tradition; St Aurelia and Merrow sustain larger research institutions.

Work

Government and services in the capital, finance and commerce in Merrow, engineering in Northmere, farming and food in Eastvale, maritime work on the coasts, energy and forestry in High Alder.

Transport

Rail links the main cities; buses and local rail serve towns; ferries are ordinary infrastructure in the islands, not a picturesque optional extra.

Faith

Religious life is part of society without becoming state theatre. Catholic institutions and traditions exist in-world alongside liberty of conscience; no fictional institution claims authority belonging to a real Church.

Sport & culture

Local clubs, national competitions, libraries, theatres, music, galleries and regional festivals provide useful reasons for citizens to care about things that are not legislation.

Voluntary life

Charities, clubs, associations and mutual aid sit between household and state. Future real-world charitable campaigns must be clearly separated from personal income and fictional public finance.

The national calendar

Seasons should change more than the background colour.

The Central Aldernia Clock can supply weekday, daypart and season to the public world. Governed calendars can later add parliamentary sittings, school terms, festivals, sports fixtures, cultural events and transport schedules.

The rule is deliberately boring and therefore valuable: time creates context, not authority. A timer can wake the system. It cannot invent a flood, a ministerial resignation or a cup final simply because five minutes have passed.

Food, weather and other constitutional essentials

A country should have things nobody wrote into the founding settlement.

Eastvale table

Orchard fruit, dairy, grain, market-garden vegetables and regional baking traditions provide the agricultural backbone of the east.

Coastal table

Fish, shellfish and preserved foods matter in Bracken and the islands. Nothing says “maritime culture” quite like discovering dinner has a tide table.

Weather

The west receives the rougher maritime weather; the eastern lowlands are gentler. Any future live weather should be clearly sourced or explicitly generated as fictional weather — never quietly confused with a real forecast.

Pubs, cafés and halls

Public life needs third places: cafés, pubs, libraries, parish halls, community centres and clubs where the state is neither absent nor the main attraction.
