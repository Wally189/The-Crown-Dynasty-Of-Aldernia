Fictional services

Public service

Institutions become believable at the handoff.

Aldernia’s public services exist to make the constitutional country legible in ordinary life: schools, health, local services, justice, libraries, transport, environmental management and emergency resilience.

Important: these are fictional institutions. They are not real hospitals, police, courts or emergency services and provide no real emergency or clinical service.

Service map

What people should be able to expect.

Education

Schools, technical education, universities and lifelong learning; public learning should remain useful outside the fiction.

Health & wellbeing

A fictional Aldernian Health Service can support stories and systems education. Any real health information requires current sources and clear real-world boundaries.

Local government

Municipal and regional institutions own genuinely local services rather than treating St Aurelia as a remote helpdesk for bins.

Justice & redress

Courts, ombudsman and complaint routes are institutionally distinct from the bodies whose decisions they review.

Libraries & knowledge

Libraries are ordinary national infrastructure: places to read, study, get help and remain in a building without being required to buy a coffee.

Resilience

Continuity and emergency planning are designed into institutions, without pretending fictional plans are real-world emergency capability.

Service standard

A form completed successfully is not necessarily a life improved.

Aldernian services are expected to make ownership, eligibility, waiting, decisions, appeals and handoffs understandable. Where one institution passes a person to another, responsibility should not evaporate in the corridor.

Success is measured by whether the intended public task can be completed accurately, accessibly and with a route to remedy — not by how many internal steps were triumphantly closed.
