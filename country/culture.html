Fictional society

Culture & society

A country needs beautiful useless things.

Choirs, bakeries, football clubs, sacred buildings, libraries, gardens, arguments about buns, local bands and village shows are not extras around “the serious institutions”. They are much of the point of having a country at all.

Liberty of conscience: Catholic Christianity is a visible historical strand of fictional Aldernian life, alongside fair treatment of other faiths and non-religious worldviews. No fictional institution creates real Church authority.

Texture

Ordinary things taken with appropriate seriousness.

Food

Hand pies, coastal cooking, orchard fruit, puddings and a civic bun dispute nobody can now remember starting.

Music

Choirs, brass bands, chamber music, indie venues and regional festivals.

Sport

Football, rugby, cricket and community sport without forcing one game to carry the entire national personality.

Places to belong

Pubs, cafés, parish halls, libraries, parks, allotments and voluntary associations.

Education & knowledge

Useful even if you do not care about the fiction.

The public world includes the University of St Aurelia, Northmere Institute of Technology, University of Merrow, St Brigid’s College, Aldernian Open College and National Library of Aldernia. They are fictional institutions and do not imply real accreditation.

Their public purpose is practical: explain constitutions, statistics, history, science, finance, systems and how to argue without pretending certainty.

Faith & freedom

Belief may shape public reasoning; it does not become compulsory.

Aldernia protects freedom of religion, freedom from religious coercion and liberty of conscience. Public Catholic content distinguishes real doctrine and real Church authority from fictional setting. The project itself makes no claim to canonical Catholic status.

Faith can appear in sacred architecture, the liturgical year, music, history, charity, conscience and moral argument without turning Government, journalism or the Crown into a simulated Church.
