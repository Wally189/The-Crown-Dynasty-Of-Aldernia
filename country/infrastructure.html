Land, water & energy

Infrastructure has a landscape.

High Alder’s forests, reservoirs and renewable energy; Eastvale’s farms and food networks; Bracken’s coast and fisheries; Southmarch’s ports and industrial settlements all create competing demands for water, housing, habitat, access, energy and investment.

Aldernian policy pages should show those trade-offs rather than producing an infrastructure plan in which every objective becomes cheaper, faster and greener simultaneously because somebody used the word “strategy”.
