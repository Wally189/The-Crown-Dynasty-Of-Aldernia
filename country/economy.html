Making a living

An economy needs more than a currency symbol.

Aldernia’s fictional mixed economy is built around knowledge and professional services, finance, digital industries, advanced manufacturing, education, creative media, maritime trade, food, energy and tourism.

The Aldernian Crown (ACR) is fictional money. It is not a cryptocurrency, investment product, payment instrument or claim on real money.

Productive geography

Different places make different things.

Trade, finance, markets, ports, professional services and commercial life.

Engineering, computing, advanced manufacturing and applied science.

Bracken & islands

Fisheries, maritime services, arts, heritage and specialist tourism.

Chamber of Commerce

Business gets a voice, not a veto.

The in-world Aldernian Chamber of Commerce convenes fictional enterprise, explains business formation and trade, surfaces barriers to investment and employment, and brings commercial evidence into public debate.

It is separate from Government: it may advocate, challenge and publish evidence, but it does not set tax, regulation, monetary policy or public spending.

Firewall: this fictional Chamber contains no real customer list, pipeline, revenue, testimonial or market proof from any real business. A fictional firm is not a lead.

The economy from four angles

Reporting starts where people and firms actually feel it.

Households

Prices, wages, housing, saving and borrowing.

Firms

Openings, closures, productivity, skills and investment.

Public choices

Fictional budgets, tax choices and spending with transparent assumptions.

Regions

Trade, infrastructure, industrial change and geographic inequality.

No GDP, inflation, tax revenue or market series is published merely to fill a dashboard. A statistic appears when its definition and provenance exist.

Economic character

Prosperity has to survive contact with a household budget.

The useful question is not whether a fictional national total looks impressive. It is whether work, skills, infrastructure, enterprise and public choices make sense together across different places.

That is why Aldernia’s economic pages treat ports, farms, engineering towns, services and household outcomes as part of one story rather than as separate dashboards.
