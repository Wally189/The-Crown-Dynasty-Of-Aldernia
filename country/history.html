History

The state is young. The places are not.

Aldernia’s founding story begins long before its present constitution: monastic sites, trading ports, guild towns, universities, farms and industrial districts developed across islands that shared culture and commerce without one fully coherent modern state.

This is fictional national history. Dates are deliberately not invented where the current canon has not established them.

Before the Compact

Several histories before one national story.

  1. Old foundationsReligious communities and settlements on St Brigid’s and elsewhere create enduring sacred, educational and cultural sites.
  2. Ports & marketsCoastal trade connects the islands; Merrow and western harbours grow through maritime commerce while Eastvale market towns develop around agriculture.
  3. Guilds & learningCraft, professional and university traditions create institutions that outlast individual governments.
  4. IndustrySouthmarch shipyards and Northmere engineering reshape work, towns and transport, leaving old industrial districts alongside newer industries.
  5. The CompactThe regions peacefully agree to create one modern realm with common institutions, national infrastructure and a constitutional Crown while preserving regional identity.
  6. The founding eraThe present public story begins provisionally: institutions are explained, challenged and completed rather than pretending the constitution arrived fully bound in calfskin.

History in the landscape

The story is easier to believe when places carry the evidence.

St Brigid’s & the islands

Old religious foundations and maritime communities make the islands one of the clearest places where sacred, educational and seafaring histories overlap.

Ports & market towns

Merrow, western harbours and Eastvale towns preserve different kinds of commercial memory: sea trade, markets, agriculture and the institutions that grew around them.

Industrial districts

Northmere engineering and Southmarch shipbuilding leave a landscape of workshops, transport links and towns shaped by work rather than by a neat national origin story.

Historical method

Memory needs labels too.

Future historical pages will distinguish settled canon, fictional archival reconstruction, legend, contested interpretation and real-world reference material. Aldernian history should acquire texture through documents, places and disagreements rather than by generating dates merely to make a timeline look authoritative.
