In-world newsroom

Public media

News should be able to surprise the Government.

Aldernia’s public-media model is deliberately separate from Government and Palace communications. It can explain national life, praise competent work, challenge grand claims and print a correction without treating the correction as a national humiliation.

Newsroom rule: publish only what the current fictional record supports. A scheduled feature may be modest; a quiet day may stay quiet; a release note is not automatically national news.

Editorial desk

Different things should look different.

News

What happened?

Report events from attributable evidence and keep the account separate from comment. A quiet day is allowed to remain quiet.

Analysis

What might it mean?

Interpret evidence while leaving uncertainty visible and separating explanation from fact.

Investigation

What withstands checking?

Test claims against records, rights of reply and proportionate publication risk.

Correction

What was wrong?

State the error and the replacement without quietly editing history.

Satire

What is obviously a joke?

Humour belongs in the public world; invented damaging factual allegations disguised as news do not.

Editorial independence

A watchdog is not required to bark at every lamppost.

Editorial independence means evidence can produce an uncomfortable story. It also means an expected scandal is not manufactured because the newsroom would like to demonstrate courage.

A claim can end as supported, unsupported, disputed, misleading, false, uncertain or genuinely unknown.

Corrections desk

Current corrections

None in this public founding edition. When material factual errors are discovered, this page will identify what was wrong and what replaced it. Updates that merely add information will be labelled as updates rather than retroactively becoming “what we always meant”.
