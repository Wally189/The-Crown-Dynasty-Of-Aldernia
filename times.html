The September Interruption · Investigation

The lights came back. The questions stayed.

Thirty-six disrupted hours on St Brigid’s Isle and the Kestrels exposed something more interesting than a storm: how quickly useful information can fail when electricity, ferries and mobile coverage all become unreliable at once.

The Times of Aldernia · House Affairs Desk · 19 September 2026

Power has been restored and ferry services are moving again. That closes the interruption. It does not close the story.

The established sequence is simple enough. Atlantic weather damaged a distribution connection serving the islands. Mobile coverage became intermittent as backup power and network congestion collided. Several ferry sailings were cancelled. Shops and public buildings improvised around charging, cash, refrigeration and information.

Then a claim began circulating that drinking water on St Brigid’s Isle had been contaminated. The claim was false. It travelled widely before the first useful official correction reached many residents.

What remains unanswered

The Government says essential services were restored within the expected resilience window. That may be true and still leave a public-information failure. The Assembly’s Infrastructure Committee has asked for the chronology of official notices, the assumptions behind communications planning and the point at which island communities were told what was known rather than what officials hoped was happening.

The Ombudsman has invited complaints about inaccessible or contradictory public notices. The Auditor-General has confirmed that any review of emergency-communications procurement will remain within its value-for-money remit rather than becoming a substitute operational inquiry.

Resilience is not the ability to declare success after the lights return. It is the ability to learn what failed while the memory is still uncomfortable.

The Times has asked the responsible departments for the publication timeline and evidence behind their claims. Their answers will be reported rather than edited on their behalf.
