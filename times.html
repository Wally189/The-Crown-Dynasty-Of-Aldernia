Founding report

The country will ask its people before calling itself finished.

Aldernia's founding constitutional model makes public participation part of the machinery rather than decoration around it.

The Times of Aldernia · House Affairs Desk · Founding period

The first Aldernian Constitution is designed to be ratified by the people who choose to join the civic electorate—not silently declared complete by the institutions that wrote it.

The proposed order separates Crown, Assembly, Government, independent constitutional institutions and specialist Houses. The Crown provides continuity. The Assembly supplies democratic authority. Government governs while it retains confidence. Teach Antony protects the electoral and constitutional process. House of Josie reports on all of them.

The principle sounds obvious until it becomes inconvenient: if a future civic ballot is opened, the system must not ask people to vote and then quietly substitute the answer institutions preferred.

That does not mean an internet poll is ready to govern Aldernia today. It is not. The public civic register remains closed while account integrity, privacy, accessibility, moderation and ballot security remain unfinished.

What readers can do now

Readers can explore the proposed Constitution, see how votes would work and challenge the assumptions behind it. Demo interactions in the present static build are explicitly not counted.

The people choose the fictional government. The system does not choose for them.

The founding country is therefore in an unusual position: intensely interested in democracy and refusing to pretend it has already solved the difficult parts.
