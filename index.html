A moment in Aldernia

Light is not time. But the speed of light is one of the rules that ties measurements of space and time together.

From tick to country

Not a Big Bang. A quickening.

This is not the story of a universe being born, or of a species evolving. It is the smaller, stranger story of a world becoming inhabited: one signal becoming a rhythm, a rhythm becoming a day, and a day acquiring a place to happen.

01

Signal

At first, a number.

Milliseconds pass with perfect indifference. The machine can count them all and still never have an evening.

02

Human time

Then someone calls it today.

The same instant acquires a date, a day, a season of life. Time stops being merely counted and starts being inhabited.

03

Place

A moment belongs somewhere.

Morning needs a window. Evening needs a street. A shared clock begins to imply weather, journeys, meals, harbours, classrooms and homes.

04

Memory

A place that remembers can make promises.

Yesterday becomes a record. A record becomes a rule. Rules become institutions. Institutions acquire duties, limits and the awkward habit of being answerable for what they do.

05

Life

Then people begin to disagree.

There are newspapers, jokes, arguments, buses missed by thirty seconds, excellent soup, bad policy, repaired policy, birthdays, storms and somebody insisting the meeting could have been an email. Now you have the beginnings of a country.
