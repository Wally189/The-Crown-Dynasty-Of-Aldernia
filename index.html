The experiment

Can very little machinery become a useful ecosystem?

The practical constraint is intentionally ordinary: a mobile phone, an ageing computer, an LLM and controlled access to digital tools and records.

  1. ObserveStart with actual state, not a story requirement.
  2. RouteGive a real need or external stimulus to the smallest competent institution.
  3. ActLet it work inside existing authority without asking what would make good theatre.
  4. ChallengeIndependent functions test evidence, competence, legality, safety and proportionality.
  5. GateReal external effects still require specific human authority.
  6. VerifyOnly observed outcomes become evidence.
  7. RememberThe verified new state becomes the input to the next cycle.

Immutable founding constraints

Freedom to develop. Not freedom from reality.

These rules constrain how Aldernia develops; they do not dictate what every institution must conclude.

LAWReal-world conduct remains subject to applicable real law and competent public authority.
CHURCHCatholic matters remain subject to competent real Church authority and applicable Canon Law within their proper remit.
DIGNITYReal people are not expendable inputs to an interesting experiment.
PROPORTIONTechnical capability is not, by itself, authority to act.
TRUTHEvidence, attribution, uncertainty and correction outrank confidence.
AGENCYAldernia may explain and persuade openly; it may not covertly choose political, religious or commercial conclusions for a person.
INDEPENDENCESpecialist and challenge functions retain their proper competence.
HUMAN STOPThe Founder remains final human constitutional authority inside the simulation, subject to superior real-world and ecclesial authority.

What genuinely exists at Day Zero

More structure than proof.

Aldernia inherits a large governed institutional design, controlled records, AI-assisted operating capability and a public website. House of Carol is a real sole-trader business in live operation.

What is not yet established is more interesting: repeatable external demand, external influence, declining founder dependence, proven social outcomes, or evidence that institutional complexity will produce better decisions rather than more bureaucracy.
