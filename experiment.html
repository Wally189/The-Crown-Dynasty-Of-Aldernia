The Aldernian Experiment

Genesis

I set out to build an online AI venture. I did not expect to end up with a Crown, a constitution and a small digital country.

I am Alan, King of the Crown Dynasty of Aldernia.

There. We may as well get that out of the way.

When I was growing up in Bristol, there was an expression occasionally deployed when somebody said something particularly unhinged: send him to Barrow Gurney.

So I appreciate that opening with the announcement that I am King of an online country does not immediately strengthen my case for continued liberty.

The important point, however, is that none of this was planned.

I did not sit down one morning and decide to create Aldernia. I wanted to build a business.

That was it.

An online business, using artificial intelligence, which I hoped might eventually make some actual money.

The business came first.

The business became House of Carol.

I had already developed a habit of separating complicated things into workstreams. It was simply how my brain worked: if a problem had six different parts, I found it easier to treat them as six different problems and then work out how they fitted together.

ChatGPT made that habit rather more consequential.

I used AI to help identify the questions: how do good businesses approach strategy? Finance? Customers? Research? Technology? Risk? Then I checked the answers against real sources and real practice.

Sometimes the AI was useful. Sometimes it was confidently wrong. Sometimes I discovered that the question itself was wrong.

But the speed was extraordinary. I could enter a field I barely knew, find its shape, identify the important sources and begin testing an idea against reality far faster than would have been practical only a few years earlier.

Then one question produced another. A business plan led to products. Products led to customers. Customers led to research, pricing, contracts and marketing. A website led to design, accessibility, privacy, security and software.

Finance wandered in. Law followed it. Quality assurance appeared shortly afterwards looking concerned.

And somewhere in the middle of all this I stopped asking ChatGPT simply to answer a question and began asking it to examine the problem from different professional positions.

What would the finance person ask?

What would the lawyer object to?

What would a researcher want evidence for?

What would quality refuse to sign off?

The answers still had to be checked. Giving an AI a job title does not magically send it to university.

But the workstreams began acquiring boundaries, responsibilities, records and relationships with one another.

I had built something too complicated for me to understand as an abstraction.
